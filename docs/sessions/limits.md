# Sessions: limits

## Limits

- Each account has its own: `claude -p /usage --output-format stream-json --verbose --no-session-persistence` in its folder every 5 minutes (a local command, spends no quota) and a statusline snapshot (marked with the session's `configDir`; without the mark — the global account); the fresher one is taken.
- Do not poll more often: the server answers `/api/oauth/usage` with 429, the VS Code extension panel polls it with the same token, and after a 429 the CLI (2.1.286) does not query this token for ~45 min.
- With an expired access token or after a 429, `/usage` returns no limits (the subscription line is there, the window lines are not) — that is "no data" (`stale`), not an error. A `/usage` answer is accepted whole or not at all: every window line it has with a percentage and a reset (`0% used` without a reset — the window has not started); a line there that does not parse rejects the answer.
- Not every plan has both windows (BTN-729). A verified Ultra (since 2026-10-30, the former Max 20x) has no five hours: `/usage` gives no "Current session" line, only "Current week (all models)" and the model's week. A Team or Enterprise seat may have no all-models week, only the session and a model's week. With one of the two lines missing the answer is read as it is: the window is `null` and listed in `RateLimits.absent`, so the page shows no meter for it (`limitSpans`), an SDK session's limits keep the mark until one reports the window (`withSessionLimits`), and a limit hit without a time on a plan without five hours waits for the week's reset (`limitResetAfter`). With neither line it is unread, as before.
- **A model's own week** (BTN-805): Max, Ultra and Team, and Pro since 2026-11-09, have a weekly limit of one model (Fable, as of 2026-11) beside the all-models week. It is a cap inside that week, not room on top of it: the model's use counts toward both, its own week is at most half of the all-models one ("Up to 50% of your weekly limit"), and a spent all-models week holds every model, this one too. Measured live on Pro: a Fable session moved its own week 0 → 4 % and the all-models week 70 → 72 % (BTN-945, cancelled for assuming the opposite). So the chain keeps holding on the all-models week whatever the task's model; the model's week only adds a hold of its own. Its text line is not parsed: the poll asks for the message stream, and the `/usage` message carries the server's rows in `usage_report.rate_limits.limits[]` (CLI 2.1.317; the same rows as `limits[]` of `/api/oauth/usage`). `modelWeeksOf` takes each `weekly_scoped` row with a model, named by its `scope.model.display_name`, into `RateLimits.models` with the poll's time; the plan's windows are still read from the text in the `result`. No `usage_report` (an older CLI, an API key) or `rate_limits: null` (the fetch failed) leaves `models` out — unknown; rows without a model week give `weeks: []` — the plan has none. A statusline snapshot and an SDK session's event do not tell model weeks, so a newer one keeps the last poll's (`newestLimits`, `withSessionLimits`); `currentLimits` drops a week past its reset and weeks read before an account switch, while a week without a reset (the server may leave it out) stays until the next poll. The page shows each week as a named meter after the plan's windows (`ModelMeter` in `web/src/layout/accountRows.tsx`). **A spent week holds the chain** (BTN-816): the next task whose model is that one waits until the week opens (`DispatcherDeps.modelWeek`, `ActiveChain.modelWeek`), while a task on another model of the same plan goes on. The server names the week by its `display_name` ("Fable") and the task's model is what the CLI takes ("fable", "claude-fable-5"), so the two are matched by the family they name (`modelWeekMatches`): a word of the name that names no family ("claude") is left out, and the rest must be a word of the model. A task whose model the CLI picks by itself (no model in the label or the project) is not matched and is not held. A week the server gave no reset for has no known end: the task waits, and only a poll can tell that the week opened; the page says the reset time is not known. A session that runs into the week itself is held as any limit is (exception 1, `docs/chain/exceptions.md`): its own refusal, with the reset from its text.
- Polling continues only if the answer says outright `num_turns: 0` and `total_cost_usd: 0`, otherwise it is switched off until restart. An unreadable answer or switched-off polling is shown by the page as a banner (`UsageBanner`). A call that fails (an exit code, a timeout) goes to the banner only when the next poll fails too: a lone one is usually the network, and it is only logged. The call closes its stdin, otherwise `-p` waits 3 s for input and prints a warning that used to be the only "reason" the banner showed.
- While a window is used up, the next task is not started: the chain waits for the reset (`limitUntil`) and continues by itself. A model's own week (Fable) used up holds the next task on that model only (`modelWeek`); the page names the week and the reset, or says that the reset time is not known.
- The windows live in the service's memory, so after a start the next task of an account that is polled waits until its poll answers (`LimitsWatcher.unread`, BTN-595): otherwise a used-up week looks like no limit for the minute the polls take, and a restart started a task straight into the limit. A lone failed call keeps the wait; two in a row (the banner) let the chain go on without limits. While it waits, the chain keeps `limitsWait` — the account and `LimitsWatcher.nextPoll` (`waitsForLimits` in `src/chain/limitNotice.ts`, the dispatcher's step and the resume after a fix of main) — and the project page shows it in a `project__wait` band (`chain.limitsUnread`, BTN-619); the band goes once the poll answers or the chain starts the task. A poll answer older than what is known (Codex reads its rollouts when ChatGPT does not answer) does not replace the newer windows.
- The newest of what a poll and an SDK session told is kept per account in the state database (`account_limits`, BTN-631) and is the watcher's first value after a start, in place of the polled or streamed windows it came from, so the usual rules of `currentLimits` (a window past its reset, `notBefore` of a switch) apply to it. A saved snapshot younger than one poll interval lifts the wait above, as it would between two polls; an older one keeps it, and until a poll answers the page marks the limits outdated with their age (`LimitsWatcher.outdated`, the `limitsOutdated` of an account entry): on the accounts page under the name and muted, in the header block and its card muted with the age.
- A limit on a running session whose text gives no reset time (the `wrap_up` warning) takes it from these limits once they are newer than the limit (`docs/chain/exceptions.md`, exception 1).
- An API account (`docs/accounts.md`) has no windows: `/usage` is not polled for it (in its folder it answers free with no window lines), and only spent credit or a spending cap is held under exception 1 (`docs/accounts.md`). Which accounts are API ones is known only once the account folders are read, so the first poll after a start waits for that (`LimitsWatcher.deferred`). The exception is an agent with `keyUsage` (Copilot): its token account is polled and waits on its limits like a subscription (`spendsByKey` in `src/limits/recovery.ts`, `keyLimits` of `SessionMonitor`).

### Limits of OpenCode and Copilot (BTN-341)

Subscription extra-usage facts are kept separately from the included windows (`RateLimits.overage`): Claude SDK rate-limit events, ChatGPT API/rollout credits and Copilot quota/billing data. They feed the display only; exhausted windows still hold the next launch as before. Sources, units, freshness and the evidence needed for an **on credits** chip are in [accounts.md](../accounts.md#subscription-credits-and-extra-usage-btn-532).

Polled every 5 minutes like `/usage`, through the adapters' `usage`; the page shows the source as "from the provider's API" (`source: 'api'`).

- **OpenCode on ChatGPT** (`src/opencode/usage.ts`, `src/limits/chatgpt.ts`): the `openai` OAuth entry of the folder's `auth.json` asks `GET chatgpt.com/backend-api/wham/usage` (`Authorization: Bearer`, `ChatGPT-Account-Id` from `accountId` or the token's `chatgpt_account_id` claim). `rate_limit.primary_window`/`secondary_window` map by `limit_window_seconds` as Codex's do (300 min, 10080, 43200). The five-minute poll is shared with Codex on the same ChatGPT account (`pollChatGpt`), including failures; an expired or refused token does not prevent a different sign-in from asking. Without a shared answer, an expired token is "no data" with `usage.chatGptExpired`; a 401/403 before its expiry, or with no known expiry, uses `usage.chatGptRevoked` (for example after a plan change). Even Keel never renews OpenCode's sign-in: OpenCode owns its tokens and renews them at its next turn. The limits carry `pool: chatgpt:<account id>`, and the cards of two accounts with one pool say "One limit with …".
- **OpenCode on Copilot**: the `github-copilot` entry's `gho_` token asks `GET api.github.com/copilot_internal/user` (`Authorization: token`). `quota_snapshots.premium_interactions` (else `chat`) with `entitlement` and `remaining` is the month, reset on `quota_reset_date`; without `remaining`, `credits_used` is subtracted from `entitlement`, including fractional AI credits. If both are present, `remaining` wins. `premium_interactions.has_quota: false`, or token-based billing without a finite positive entitlement, is "no data" with `usage.copilotNoQuota`, before considering unlimited or another quota. Invalid or negative numeric readings are unread. Copilot Free's `limited_user_quotas.chat` out of `monthly_quotas.chat` stands in when there are no readable snapshots; an unlimited snapshot is "no data" with `usage.copilotUnlimited`.
- **OpenCode Go** (BTN-803, `src/opencode/go.ts`): the Console key in `auth.json`, under `opencode-go` then legacy `opencode`, asks `GET https://opencode.ai/zen/go/v1/usage` with `Authorization: Bearer`. `usage.rolling`, `weekly`, `monthly` map to `fiveHour`, `sevenDay`, `month`. All three windows must have a finite nonnegative `percent` and a valid ISO reset with a timezone; percentages stay on the assumed 0–100 scale, even below one. An incomplete or malformed answer is unread (`usage.goUnread`); network/429/5xx failures use the usual two-failure handling. 403 means no Go subscription (`usage.openCodeNone`); 401 clears the polled windows and asks for a new Console key through Replace key (`usage.goKeyExpired`), never a sign-in renewal. Existing snapshot persistence and first-poll gating apply after a restart. All-API Console folders use `authMethod: subscription_key` so usage is polled rather than treated as per-token spend; no key is sent to OpenAI's API-key checker. Mixed folders keep the existing ChatGPT → Copilot precedence, then Console. The process-wide `OPENCODE_API_KEY` is not a credential of an isolated folder. OpenCode 2's credential database is not read yet. Provider behaviour and the percent scale remain [unverified](../unverified/opencode.md#opencode-go-limits-btn-803).
- **OpenCode without ChatGPT, Copilot or a Console key** — "no data" with `usage.openCodeNone`, shown instead of empty window rows. Such a folder is known to have nothing to read before any poll (`Agent.limitsHidden`, `openCodeLimitsHidden`), so a chain on it does not wait for its first poll (`LimitsWatcher.unread`, BTN-738); it is still polled for the card's line. The free models have OpenCode's own usage limit, seen only from a session's refusal. A Go refusal saying "usage limit reached. It will reset in …" uses its days, hours, minutes and seconds from the refusal timestamp (`resetTimeOf`), through the existing limit wait. An unreadable duration keeps the ordinary retry mechanism; no new stop or resume path is added.
- **Copilot with a token** (`askCopilotToken` in `src/limits/copilot.ts`): `copilot_internal/user` with `Bearer` first; if it refuses or is unreadable, `GET /user` gives the login and `/users/<login>/settings/billing/premium_request/usage` the month's `grossQuantity`. An empty `usageItems` (or one with no recognized product), or HTTP 404, tries `/users/<login>/settings/billing/ai_credit/usage`. The products `Copilot`, `Copilot AI Credits` and `AI credits` are accepted case-insensitively, including for the separate historical paid-usage total; unknown products are logged and ignored. Reports are alternatives, never added together. That count has no allowance: the window is `used` with 0 %, never full, and only gives the reset on the 1st. A final empty report is "no data" with `usage.copilotBillingEmpty`; an explicit zero quantity is a reading. Malformed reports remain unread; 403, or 404 on the final endpoint, uses `usage.planRead`. Network/429/5xx failures retain the ordinary poll failure handling. [GitHub's REST documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/billing/usage#get-billing-ai-credit-usage-report-for-a-user) lists fine-grained PATs with "Plan" read permission for AI credit reports (checked 2026-11-12); no token-creation change is needed. The machine's sign-in is "no data" with `usage.copilotMachine`: the GitHub CLI token cannot be attributed to the Copilot CLI account, so Even Keel does not send it to the usage API. Neither token is put in the session environment or Even Keel state. Existing polling, snapshot persistence and restart gating apply unchanged.
- A month that counts what is `left` lets a limit go on at once while something is left (`limitResetAfter`), since GitHub's quota and a rate limit share one error kind; otherwise a limit or spent credit waits for the month's reset.
- A refusal whose text names the monthly quota (Copilot's `quota_exceeded`, "You have exceeded your monthly quota", `isMonthQuota`) spends the month at once (`LimitsWatcher.spendMonth`, BTN-647): until a newer poll the account shows 0 left and the hold takes the month's reset, rather than "reset unknown" off a poll taken minutes before the refusal. A Copilot token never gets the page's "out of credit" text: without a reset it is the plain "reset unknown".

### Answers of plans we have no account for (BTN-609)

The author has Claude Pro and ChatGPT Plus; no plans are bought for checks. The limit answers of the other plans (Claude Max, Ultra, Team; ChatGPT Pro; paid Copilot; OpenCode Go) come from public GitHub issues and the MIT code of tools that show subscription limits: their users paste the raw answer with the plan named, and a new shape shows up there within days of a provider's change. Searches that found them on 2026-11-02:

- `gh search issues '"weekly_scoped"'`, `'"model_scoped"'`, `'"five_hour" null'`, `'"api/oauth/usage"'` — Claude;
- `'"wham/usage"'`, `'"x-codex-primary-used-percent"'` — ChatGPT and Codex;
- `'"quota_snapshots" premium_interactions'`, `'"premium_request/usage"'` — Copilot;
- `'FreeUsageLimitError'`, `'"OpenCode Go" limit'` — OpenCode.

A sample made from such an answer is written by us in the shape of the source, never copied as it is, with made-up values in place of ids, emails and dates, and with fields the source cut out left out rather than invented. It goes into `test/fixtures/limits/` with its plan, date and source link in that folder's `README.md`. The behaviour it backs stays in `docs/unverified/` until it is seen live. The sources found so far are listed in the closing comment of BTN-609.
