# Sessions: limits

## Limits

### Claude's poll

- Each account has its own: `claude -p /usage --output-format stream-json --verbose --no-session-persistence` in its folder every 5 minutes (a local command, spends no quota) and a statusline snapshot (marked with the session's `configDir`; without the mark — the global account); the fresher one is taken.
- A Claude folder on a long token (BTN-1005, `docs/accounts.md`, "Long-token accounts") is not polled: `/usage` has no windows for it. Its limits are only what its sessions report, or those of the full sign-in folder it was said to be the same account as; a machine's token folder tied to a hub account (BTN-1002) gets that account's polled limits from the hub.
- One claude.ai account signed in on several machines of a hub (by `oauthAccount` of `.claude.json`) is polled in one place, the hub's own machine first; the others skip their folder of it and take the newest limits through the hub (BTN-991, `docs/machines/limits.md`, "The account and its poller").
- Do not poll more often: the server answers `/api/oauth/usage` with 429, the VS Code extension panel polls it with the same token, and after a 429 the CLI (2.1.286) does not query this token for ~45 min.
- **The guard.** `/usage` must stay a local command: polling continues only if the answer says outright `num_turns: 0` and `total_cost_usd: 0`. An answer that took a model turn or cost money switches polling off until restart (`spentOrFailed` in `src/limits/usage.ts`). An unreadable answer or switched-off polling is shown by the page as a banner (`UsageBanner`). A call that fails (an exit code, a timeout) goes to the banner only when the next poll fails too: a lone one is usually the network, and it is only logged. The call closes its stdin, otherwise `-p` waits 3 s for input and prints a warning.

### What the answer holds

- **The server's rows** (EKR-131, CLI 2.1.317): the `/usage` message of the stream carries `usage_report.rate_limits.limits[]`, the same rows as `limits[]` of `/api/oauth/usage`. Both the plan's windows and a model's own week are read from them:
  - `planWindowsOf` takes the `kind: session` row as the five hours and `kind: weekly_all` as the all-models week, each with its `percent` and `resets_at`; an unused row with `resets_at: null` is a window not started. Every row counts, whatever its `is_active`: that flag marks the one limit holding the account now, not the limits that apply. Pro has both rows (seen 2026-11-17 in the CLI's own cache, `cachedUsageUtilization` of `.claude.json`, with `is_active` on the session only).
  - `modelWeeksOf` takes each `weekly_scoped` row with a model, named by its `scope.model.display_name`, into `RateLimits.models` with the poll's time (the next section).
- **The text** of the `result` is the fallback when the answer has no `usage_report` (an older CLI), `rate_limits: null` (the fetch failed), neither window row, or a row no window can be made of (no `percent`, a used row without a reset). It is accepted whole or not at all: every window line it has with a percentage and a reset (`0% used` without a reset — the window has not started); a line there that does not parse rejects the answer.
- With an expired access token or after a 429, `/usage` returns no limits (the subscription line is there, the window lines are not) — that is "no data" (`stale`), not an error.
- **Not every plan has both windows** (BTN-729). A verified Ultra (since 2026-10-30, the former Max 20x) has no five hours: no `session` row (`five_hour: null` in `/api/oauth/usage`), and in the text no "Current session" line, only "Current week (all models)" and the model's week. A Team or Enterprise seat may have no all-models week (no `weekly_all` row, no such line), only the session and a model's week. With one of the two missing the answer is read as it is: the window is `null` and listed in `RateLimits.absent`, so the page shows no meter for it (`limitSpans`), an SDK session's limits keep the mark until one reports the window (`withSessionLimits`), and a limit hit without a time on a plan without five hours waits for the week's reset (`limitResetAfter`). With neither row nor line it is unread.

### A model's own week (BTN-805, BTN-816)

- **What it is.** Max, Ultra and Team, and Pro since 2026-11-09, have a weekly limit of one model (Fable, as of 2026-11) beside the all-models week. It is a cap inside that week, not room on top of it: the model's use counts toward both, its own week is at most half of the all-models one ("Up to 50% of your weekly limit"), and a spent all-models week holds every model, this one too. Measured live on Pro: a Fable session moved its own week 0 → 4 % and the all-models week 70 → 72 % (BTN-945). So the chain keeps holding on the all-models week whatever the task's model; the model's week only adds a hold of its own.
- **Reading.** Its text line is not parsed; it comes only from the rows. No `usage_report` (an older CLI, an API key) or `rate_limits: null` leaves `models` out — unknown; rows without a model week give `weeks: []` — the plan has none. A statusline snapshot and an SDK session's event do not tell model weeks, so a newer one keeps the last poll's (`newestLimits`, `withSessionLimits`); `currentLimits` drops a week past its reset and weeks read before an account switch, while a week without a reset (the server may leave it out) stays until the next poll. The page shows each week as a named meter after the plan's windows (`ModelMeter` in `web/src/layout/accountRows.tsx`).
- **The hold.** A spent week holds the next task whose model is that one until the week opens (`DispatcherDeps.modelWeek`, `ActiveChain.modelWeek`); a task on another model of the same plan goes on. The page names the week and the reset, or says the reset time is not known: a week the server gave no reset for has no known end, and only a poll can tell that it opened.
- **Matching the model.** The server names the week by its `display_name` ("Fable") and the task's model is what the CLI takes ("fable", "claude-fable-5"), so the two are matched by the family they name (`modelWeekMatches`): a word of the name that names no family ("claude") is left out, and the rest must be a word of the model. A task whose model the CLI picks by itself (no model in the label or the project) is not matched and is not held.
- A session that runs into the week itself is held as any limit is (exception 1, `docs/chain/exceptions.md`): its own refusal, with the reset from its text.

### The chain and the limits

- While a window is used up, the next task is not started: the chain waits for the reset (`limitUntil`) and continues by itself.
- While the current task's account has a window used up (`machine.limitReset`, what `requireLimit` refuses a new session on), its lane in the page's state carries `spent` (`spentLimit` in `src/chain/limitNotice.ts`, `ActiveChainView`, BTN-1359): the session panel holds "New session" with a tip of the reset and the account, named as the `chain.limitSpent` refusal names them, and that the chain hands the task over by itself; "Skip" stays. A poll that frees a window drops `spent` with the next state, so the button comes back without a reload. The refusal stays for a race.
- The windows live in the service's memory, so after a start the next task of an account that is polled waits until its poll answers (`LimitsWatcher.unread`, BTN-595): otherwise a used-up week looks like no limit for the minute the polls take, and a restart started a task straight into the limit. A lone failed call keeps the wait; two in a row (the banner) let the chain go on without limits. While it waits, the chain keeps `limitsWait` — the account and `LimitsWatcher.nextPoll` (`waitsForLimits` in `src/chain/limitNotice.ts`, the dispatcher's step and the resume after a fix of main) — and the project page shows it in the chain's strip under its buttons, "waiting" with the account and the next poll in its tip (`chain.limitsUnread`, BTN-619, BTN-1162); it goes once the poll answers or the chain starts the task. A lane on a machine elsewhere reads the wait of that machine's account through its mirror (`limitsUnread` of `MachineFacts`) and shows it the same way (BTN-1521, `src/e2e/elsewhere.test.ts`). A poll answer older than what is known (Codex reads its rollouts when ChatGPT does not answer) does not replace the newer windows.
- **Sessions report between the polls** (`AgentOptions.onLimits` → `LimitsWatcher.report`, `withSessionLimits`): a Claude SDK session at each `rate_limit_event` (BTN-290), a Codex session at each `token_count` of its rollout with windows (the next section). A Codex report carries its rollout line's time, a Claude SDK report the time it arrived; one older than the newest the account knows is passed over, so a rollout read at a start does not roll back a poll or a newer report. A report keeps what it does not tell: the model weeks, a seat's cap and month (`newestLimits`), the month and the ChatGPT `pool`; `open: false` of a poll stays, `open: true` does not pass into a session snapshot (`source: 'sdk'`), so an early reset is still the poll's alone (`limitReopenedAt`). The five-minute poll stays as it is for the time no session runs.
- The newest of what a poll and an SDK session told is kept per account in the state database (`account_limits`, BTN-631) and is the watcher's first value after a start, in place of the polled or streamed windows it came from, so the usual rules of `currentLimits` (a window past its reset, `notBefore` of a switch) apply to it. A saved snapshot younger than one poll interval lifts the wait above, as it would between two polls; an older one keeps it, and until a poll answers the page marks the limits outdated with their age (`LimitsWatcher.outdated`, the `limitsOutdated` of an account entry): on the accounts page under the name and muted, in the header block and its card muted with the age.
- A limit on a running session whose text gives no reset time (the `wrap_up` warning) takes it from these limits once they are newer than the limit (`docs/chain/exceptions.md`, exception 1).
- An API account (`docs/accounts.md`) has no windows: `/usage` is not polled for it (in its folder it answers free with no window lines), and only spent credit or a spending cap is held under exception 1 (`docs/accounts.md`). Which accounts are API ones is known only once the account folders are read, so the first poll after a start waits for that (`LimitsWatcher.deferred`). The exception is an agent with `keyUsage` (Copilot): its token account is polled and waits on its limits like a subscription (`spendsByKey` in `src/limits/recovery.ts`, `keyLimits` of `SessionMonitor`).

## ChatGPT (Codex and OpenCode)

A ChatGPT sign-in has the same limits whichever agent uses it (`pollChatGpt` in `src/limits/chatgpt.ts`): Codex reads its sign-in through `CodexUsage` (`src/codex/usage.ts`), OpenCode through `src/opencode/usage.ts`.

- **The poll.** `GET chatgpt.com/backend-api/wham/usage` with `Authorization: Bearer` and `ChatGPT-Account-Id`: for Codex `tokens.access_token` and `tokens.account_id` of the folder's `auth.json`, for OpenCode the `openai` OAuth entry of its `auth.json` (`accountId`, or the token's `chatgpt_account_id` claim). Codex and OpenCode share a five-minute poll by `chatgpt:<account id>`, including an in-flight request and failures; a refused token may be retried with another folder's sign-in. The limits carry `pool: chatgpt:<account id>`, and the cards of two accounts with one pool say "One limit with …".
- **Windows by length.** `rate_limit.primary_window`/`secondary_window` (and a rollout's `rate_limits`) map by `limit_window_seconds`: 300 minutes to five hours, 10080 to a week, 43200 to a month (`RateLimits.month`; the free plan has only that one, `plan_type: "free"`, seen on Codex 0.162.1); a window of another length is not shown. A plan with a month shows it in place of the windows it lacks (`limitSpans`), the chain counts a spent month like a spent week, and a limit hit on it waits for the month's reset. Tally does not snapshot the month (`span` is `5h`/`7d` only).
- **Renewing Codex's token.** On a 401/403 before the access token's JWT `exp`, or with no known expiry, `CodexUsage` asks Codex's app-server for `account/read` with `refreshToken: true` once, rereads `auth.json` and retries with the replacement token. Codex alone writes the tokens; no thread or turn is started. Renewal waits while the folder has any live Even Keel session, resting ones included (`isLive`); launches and resumes wait for an ongoing renewal too. A failed renewal or a refused replacement is not renewed again in that service run; a successful API answer clears that hold.
- **OpenCode's token is never renewed** by Even Keel: OpenCode owns its tokens and renews them at its next turn. Without a shared answer, an expired token is "no data" with `usage.chatGptExpired`; a 401/403 before its expiry, or with no known expiry, uses `usage.chatGptRevoked` (for example after a plan change).
- **The rollout fallback (Codex).** A refused or unreadable answer or a network failure falls back to `rate_limits` of the newest `token_count` in the account's latest rollouts; without either source there is no data, keeping the expiry or revocation reason when known. API-key folders keep the rollout path (the watcher does not poll them). A `token_count` whose `rate_limits` have no window is passed over: 0.164.0 writes one with `limit_id: "premium"` and `primary`/`secondary` both `null` right before a turn refused by the limit, and taken it would wipe the spent window.
- **The reset from a refusal.** The refusal is the `error` of `task_complete` (`codex_error_info: "usage_limit_exceeded"`), and its "try again at Nov 16th, 2026 10:12 PM" (a bare "3:46 PM" for the same day, in the machine's zone) is the limit's reset (`resetTimeOf`), before any window says so. The time is formatted from the server's `resets_at` with the seconds cut off, so a bare time is the same local date as the refusal, and a passed one is the rounding of the current minute, not tomorrow (EKR-34: a session started at 12:55:06 for "12:55 PM" was refused with the same time, and the chain waited a day). The bare time of Claude's "resets 1am" still rolls over to the next day. The wait ends a minute after the reset, and a refusal naming a reset over a minute gone waits 10 minutes from the refusal (`releaseAt`, `src/chain/relay.ts`).
- **An early reset** (BTN-530, `docs/chain.md`): an API answer after the refusal with `rate_limit.allowed: true` and `limit_reached: false` (`RateLimits.open`) ends the wait before the named reset. A rollout's limits carry no such flag and are judged by their windows, as Claude's `/usage` is.
- **A live Codex session reports its rollout's limits** (BTN-1320): the poll lags the site by up to five minutes, and a heavy model spends about 10 % of the five hours in that time (2026-11-18: the page showed 95 % when the session was refused at 100 %). So the session's `RolloutTail` folds `rate_limits` of each `token_count` as `limitsOfRollout` does (`RolloutFold.limits`, the same windows by length, the windowless `premium` ones passed over) and, after a read that brings newer ones, hands them to `onLimits` about every 10 s while it works, with the line's time and the folder's `pool` (`sessionLimitsOf`, `tellLimits` in `src/codex/adapter.ts`); an OpenCode folder on the same ChatGPT account sees them through the pool. The rollout names every window the plan has, so one it lacks is `absent` (Pro's five hours), not the polled one. A report of no shown window is not made.
- **A workspace seat** (EKR-132, `src/limits/chatgpt-seat.ts`): `wham/usage` with `spend_control` and a `plan_type` other than free, Go, Plus and Pro. Unverified: `docs/unverified/accounts.md`.
  - When `spend_control.individual_limit` is there in credits (a Business seat with a per-seat limit, EKR-152; one public issue so far, `test/fixtures/limits/README.md`), its `used` of `limit` — decimal strings — is the month, resetting at its own `reset_at` (else `reset_after_seconds`), and nothing more is asked (`ownSeatMonth`).
  - Otherwise (`individual_limit: null`, as on the Edu seat and an Enterprise seat with no cap) the same poll asks `GET chatgpt.com/backend-api/accounts/<account id>/spend-controls/current-user/monthly-usage?supports_usage_limit_modes=true` with the same headers — Codex never does, ChatGPT's spend page does — and its `current_month_usage` of `effective_monthly_limit.limit` credits becomes the month (`LimitWindow.used`/`limit`, the card's "1250 of 5000"), resetting at the next calendar month in UTC; a seat without a cap shows only what it used. A refusal or failure of that request leaves the month out, never fails the poll.
  - Whether the seat is closed is `spend_control.reached`'s alone (`RateLimits.spendCap`): it makes `open` false and the month 100 % (a month of its own when the count was not read), so the chain holds the account as on a spent month, a held session goes on once a poll says it is no longer reached, and the card shows `usage.chatGptSpendCap` (the answer's `reason`, kept in the account's usage health). Short of it the count stays under 100 % even past the cap.
  - A newer session snapshot keeps the cap and its month (`newestLimits`), and the seat's month is no paid-usage evidence (`overageLimit`): its credits are the cap itself.

## Other agents' limits (BTN-341)

Polled every 5 minutes like `/usage`, through the adapters' `usage`; the page shows the source as "from the provider's API" (`source: 'api'`).

- **OpenCode on ChatGPT** — the previous section.
- **OpenCode on Copilot**: the `github-copilot` entry's `gho_` token asks `GET api.github.com/copilot_internal/user` (`Authorization: token`). `quota_snapshots.premium_interactions` (else `chat`) with `entitlement` and `remaining` is the month, reset on `quota_reset_date`; without `remaining`, `credits_used` is subtracted from `entitlement`, including fractional AI credits. If both are present, `remaining` wins. `premium_interactions.has_quota: false`, or token-based billing without a finite positive entitlement, is "no data" with `usage.copilotNoQuota`, before considering unlimited or another quota. Invalid or negative numeric readings are unread. Copilot Free's `limited_user_quotas.chat` out of `monthly_quotas.chat` stands in when there are no readable snapshots; an unlimited snapshot is "no data" with `usage.copilotUnlimited`. A flag the quota contradicts is not unlimited (EKR-133): `unlimited: true` with `remaining` or `entitlement` 0, `credits_used` above zero and `overage_permitted: false` is a spent month to `quota_reset_date`; with overage permitted the same shape stays unlimited.
- **OpenCode Go** (BTN-803, `src/opencode/go.ts`): the Console key in `auth.json`, under `opencode-go` then legacy `opencode`, asks `GET https://opencode.ai/zen/go/v1/usage` with `Authorization: Bearer`. `usage.rolling`, `weekly`, `monthly` map to `fiveHour`, `sevenDay`, `month`.
  - All three windows must have a finite nonnegative `percent` and a valid ISO reset with a timezone; percentages stay on the assumed 0–100 scale, even below one. An incomplete or malformed answer is unread (`usage.goUnread`); network/429/5xx failures use the usual two-failure handling. 403 means no Go subscription (`usage.openCodeNone`); 401 clears the polled windows and asks for a new Console key through Replace key (`usage.goKeyExpired`), never a sign-in renewal. Snapshot persistence and first-poll gating apply after a restart.
  - All-API Console folders use `authMethod: subscription_key` so usage is polled rather than treated as per-token spend; no key is sent to OpenAI's API-key checker. Mixed folders keep the ChatGPT → Copilot precedence, then Console. The process-wide `OPENCODE_API_KEY` is not a credential of an isolated folder. OpenCode 2's credential database is not read yet.
  - A Go refusal saying "usage limit reached. It will reset in …" uses its days, hours, minutes and seconds from the refusal timestamp (`resetTimeOf`), through the usual limit wait. An unreadable duration keeps the ordinary retry.
  - Provider behaviour and the percent scale remain [unverified](../unverified/opencode.md#opencode-go-limits-btn-803).
- **OpenCode without ChatGPT, Copilot or a Console key** — "no data" with `usage.openCodeNone`, shown instead of empty window rows. Such a folder is known to have nothing to read before any poll (`Agent.limitsHidden`, `openCodeLimitsHidden`), so a chain on it does not wait for its first poll (`LimitsWatcher.unread`, BTN-738); it is still polled for the card's line. The free models have OpenCode's own usage limit, seen only from a session's refusal.
- **Copilot with a token** (`askCopilotToken` in `src/limits/copilot.ts`): `copilot_internal/user` with `Bearer` first; if it refuses or is unreadable, `GET /user` gives the login and `/users/<login>/settings/billing/premium_request/usage` the month's `grossQuantity`. An empty `usageItems` (or one with no recognized product), or HTTP 404, tries `/users/<login>/settings/billing/ai_credit/usage`. The products `Copilot`, `Copilot AI Credits` and `AI credits` are accepted case-insensitively, including for the separate historical paid-usage total; unknown products are logged and ignored. Reports are alternatives, never added together. That count has no allowance: the window is `used` with 0 %, never full, and only gives the reset on the 1st. A final empty report is "no data" with `usage.copilotBillingEmpty`; an explicit zero quantity is a reading. Malformed reports remain unread; 403, or 404 on the final endpoint, uses `usage.planRead`. Network/429/5xx failures retain the ordinary poll failure handling. [GitHub's REST documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/billing/usage#get-billing-ai-credit-usage-report-for-a-user) lists fine-grained PATs with "Plan" read permission for AI credit reports (checked 2026-11-12); no token-creation change is needed. The machine's sign-in is "no data" with `usage.copilotMachine`: the GitHub CLI token cannot be attributed to the Copilot CLI account, so Even Keel does not send it to the usage API. Neither token is put in the session environment or Even Keel state.
- A month that counts what is `left` lets a limit go on at once while something is left (`limitResetAfter`), since GitHub's quota and a rate limit share one error kind; otherwise a limit or spent credit waits for the month's reset.
- A refusal whose text names the monthly quota (Copilot's `quota_exceeded`, "You have exceeded your monthly quota", `isMonthQuota`) spends the month at once (`LimitsWatcher.spendMonth`, BTN-647): until a newer poll the account shows 0 left and the hold takes the month's reset, rather than "reset unknown" off a poll taken minutes before the refusal. A Copilot token never gets the page's "out of credit" text: without a reset it is the plain "reset unknown".

**Extra usage** of a subscription is kept apart from the included windows (`RateLimits.overage`): Claude SDK rate-limit events, ChatGPT API/rollout credits and Copilot quota/billing data. It feeds the display only; exhausted windows still hold the next launch. Sources, units, freshness and the evidence needed for an **on credits** chip are in [accounts.md](../accounts/page.md#subscription-credits-and-extra-usage-btn-532).

## Answers of plans we have no account for (BTN-609)

The author has Claude Pro and ChatGPT Plus; no plans are bought for checks. The limit answers of the other plans (Claude Max, Ultra, Team; ChatGPT Pro; paid Copilot; OpenCode Go) come from public GitHub issues and the MIT code of tools that show subscription limits: their users paste the raw answer with the plan named, and a new shape shows up there within days of a provider's change. Every release starts with a search since the last one (`docs/releases.md`, "A release"); the last was on 2026-11-20 (EKR-152: the per-seat credit limit of a ChatGPT business seat in `wham/usage`); the one before on 2026-11-17 (EKR-131, EKR-132, EKR-133). Searches that found them on 2026-11-02:

- `gh search issues '"weekly_scoped"'`, `'"model_scoped"'`, `'"five_hour" null'`, `'"api/oauth/usage"'` — Claude;
- `'"wham/usage"'`, `'"x-codex-primary-used-percent"'` — ChatGPT and Codex;
- `'"quota_snapshots" premium_interactions'`, `'"premium_request/usage"'` — Copilot;
- `'FreeUsageLimitError'`, `'"OpenCode Go" limit'` — OpenCode.

A sample made from such an answer is written by us in the shape of the source, never copied as it is, with made-up values in place of ids, emails and dates, and with fields the source cut out left out rather than invented. It goes into `test/fixtures/limits/` with its plan, date and source link in that folder's `README.md`, which lists the sources found so far. The behaviour it backs stays in `docs/unverified/` until it is seen live.
