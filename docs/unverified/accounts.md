## Paid subscription usage stops

BTN-937 separates the stop log's confirmed/provider-supported signal from Claude's `/usage` inference, and clarifies the page's policy tip. Unit tests cover both wordings, missing evidence after a restart, and the unchanged retained ban and reset recovery. The new service-log wording still needs observation on a bench; the inferred stop below was observed with the old wording.

BTN-577 stops a managed chain's current live session after fresh direct or inferred paid-usage evidence, retains a limit hold and handoff, and waits for included allowance or another usable account. Fixture tests cover each provider source, freshness, historical/unknown data, shared ChatGPT pools, stop/wait/reset/account switch, paused chains, warm/cold recovery and service-restart snapshots. The warning on account cards was inspected with fake data ([live log](../verified/accounts.md)). The inferred Claude stop is seen live, read from the service log and Tally's limit snapshots ([live log](../verified/accounts.md)): on 2026-10-30…11-09 seven chain sessions on three subscription accounts were stopped 20–30 s after a `/usage` poll read a five-hour or weekly window at 100 % while they worked, one of them a `--bg` turn cut in the middle of a tool call; none was started again on the spent account, and each task went on — a warm resume of the same session a minute after the five-hour reset, a cold handoff right at the weekly reset, or a handoff to another account within seconds — and closed. Whether those accounts had extra usage turned on is not known: these stops prove the stop and the recovery, not that money was being spent. Not seen: the SDK's direct `isUsingOverage`, the ChatGPT and Copilot sources, and a restart of the service during the wait.

Manual task BTN-580, related to source check BTN-531, requires separate explicit consent before any paid request. Check Claude SDK direct `isUsingOverage`, Codex/OpenCode on ChatGPT with positive credits and an exhausted window, and Copilot/OpenCode on Copilot with exhausted monthly allowance and `overage_permitted`. For each, confirm the stop of the agent's own process, no external session touched and no immediate relaunch (seen for Claude `--bg` above), and a service restart during the wait or handoff. Switching within a shared ChatGPT pool must still wait. Unknown resets stay unknown; stale evidence, historical totals and a positive balance alone must not stop a session. Do not increase usage polling to create the scenario.

Assumed: provider signals arrive in the observed shapes and each agent stops its real managed process. If wrong, spending may continue, a session may stop early, or recovery may lose context or relaunch while exhausted. A successful stop cannot undo charges before detection; SDK events follow requests and polls can lag by five minutes. **Workaround:** disable extra usage or automatic credit reload, or set a provider monthly spending cap where supported; stop/pause the chain and inspect the provider's usage before continuing. Pausing does not prevent detection from stopping a session already working, but holds automatic continuation. API-key policies and unknown Zen balances are unchanged.

## Staying signed in after a reboot (EKR-15)

On 21.10.2026 a reboot signed two of three subscription accounts out: the service started before the network, every account's `claude auth status` timed out at once, and a minute later two `.credentials.json` held neither an access nor a refresh token. The CLI renews with a single-use refresh token, so Even Keel assumes the sign-in was lost to a renewal cut off half-way or run twice at once, and guards against both: it asks no account and no `/usage` until the provider's API answers, runs its short calls of one folder one at a time and does not kill one on a timeout (`docs/accounts.md`). Neither the cause nor the guard has been seen live: a reboot with expired tokens and no network at the start was not repeated. If an account still asks to sign in after a reboot, the cause is elsewhere. **Workaround:** sign the account in again from the accounts drawer.

## Claude OAuth token emptying diagnostics (BTN-763)

Unit tests cover the credential shape and transitions, first readings and restarts, repeated and concurrent checks, global folders, previous expiry times, peers within five seconds, invalid JSON and absent OAuth, failed diagnostic reads, and omission of token values. Assumed: a valid `.credentials.json` observed before `auth status` retains the CLI's write time and has string access/refresh fields, including empty strings when the CLI clears them. If wrong, a transition can be missed or read on a later poll; a clear followed by a sign-in between polls cannot be seen. **Check at the next natural sign-out:** preserve the `account: ...: the CLI emptied the OAuth tokens at ...` line in `logs/even-keel.log`; compare its millisecond mtime, previous expiry dates, CLI version, live PIDs and nearby folder keys with independent file/process evidence, without copying any token text. Confirm one line across repeated checks and a `signed in again` line after normal sign-in. If the service starts with tokens already empty, expect only `OAuth tokens empty since`. **Workaround:** sign in again from Accounts and preserve the log; this diagnostic neither repairs credentials nor establishes whether the server revoked a token or processes raced.

## A Claude plan change (BTN-393)

A ChatGPT plan change revokes the sign-in's access token at once: on 25.10.2026, right after free → Plus, a Codex folder's token with ten days left got 401 from ChatGPT's usage answer until Codex renewed it, and the card had no bars until then. Whether moving a Claude subscription between Pro and Max does the same to the CLI's OAuth token has never been seen. Even Keel assumes the sign-in keeps working and the card takes the new plan and windows at the next `auth status` and `/usage`. If the token is revoked, the card may keep the old plan or show no bars until the CLI renews the token at its next call, or the account may ask to sign in. **Workaround:** start a session on the account, or sign it in again from the accounts drawer.

## Settings edits in account folders (BTN-901)

The transfer keeps a top-level key changed right in an account folder's `settings.json` and the page asks what to do with it (`docs/accounts.md`, "Transferring the shared configuration"). Covered by unit tests (`settingsLayer.test.ts`: edits, the held value, the layer, `env` without the folder's own variables, the silent keys; the watcher's commands; the database step) and the fake page (`?account=settings`). Seen only passively (2026-11-09, [verified](../verified/accounts.md)): in the first day after it was deployed, with the service started many times and dozens of chain sessions on four Claude folders, no folder showed an edit. Not run: a hand edit, the band's buttons, a CLI update. Assumed: the CLI writes no other key into `settings.json` on its own than `autoUpdatesChannel` and `minimumVersion` (read in the 2.1.319 binary); its startup migrations of `model` (renames such as `opus` → `opus[1m]`, the org default cleared) and of `env` (`DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER`, `ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_*_MODEL` pins) run rarely enough to be worth a band, since a person writes those keys too. If wrong, the band shows a change nobody made after a CLI update or a start, and "Discard" has to be pressed for it. **Check (BTN-904):** in an account folder, `/model` in a terminal session and a hand edit of `skillOverrides`; the band shows each key, and each of the three buttons does what it says on the next launch, on the folder and on `~/.claude/settings.json`; "Own settings" on `/accounts` lists the kept key and × brings the shared value back; a CLI update and a `claude install` raise no band. **Workaround:** make the change in `~/.claude/settings.json` instead.
