# What has not been verified: Claude limits

One area of [What has not been verified](../unverified.md), which says what an entry is, how to report a run into one and where a new one goes. What has been checked in this area is in [What has been verified: Claude limits](../verified/claude-limits.md).

## An early reset of a limit (BTN-530)

Anthropic and OpenAI sometimes reset a window or all limits of an account ahead of time (OpenAI's weekly Plus limit kept returning to 100% in late October 2026). When a session hits a limit, the chain waits for the reset named by the error, and also ends the wait when a poll taken after the refusal shows the account open again: for ChatGPT, `wham/usage` with `rate_limit.allowed: true` and `limit_reached: false`; for Claude, a `/usage` reading with no window at 100% and none resetting within an hour of the named time, as a window started over resets later. It then resumes the session or hands over, as at the reset. A poll taken less than 10 minutes before the named reset does not count: on 2026-10-31 `/usage` showed the five-hour window unstarted at 00:48 UTC while the CLI refused until 00:50, and the chain started a session into the refusal. An end-to-end scenario covers the chain's part with the scripted agent's `/usage` (`src/e2e/limits.test.ts`, BTN-663): a session refused until 13:00, a poll at 09:40 showing the five-hour window unstarted, the same session resumed within one poll and the log line on the early return. What either real answer looks like right after an early reset has never been seen. If ChatGPT says `allowed` while the account is still refused, the session is refused again and waits for the next named reset; if Claude's `/usage` keeps the old window or its reset after an early reset, the chain keeps waiting until the original reset. **Workaround:** press "New session" on the task — it drops the remembered wait and starts a session at once; or switch the account.

## The first limits poll after a restart (BTN-595)

The windows are known only in memory, so after a start of the service the next task waits until its account's first limits poll answers; before this a restart started a Codex task into a used-up week. The wait is covered by tests, and an end-to-end scenario runs it through a restart of the service (`src/e2e/limits.test.ts`, BTN-663): with the first poll held, the next task waits; a spent window keeps it out until the reset, a free one lets it go within seconds of the answer. Seen once after a real restart (2026-11-01, [verified.md](../verified/claude-limits.md)): a Copilot task waited 39 s for the first allowance poll and started on a free window. Not seen: a spent window held after a restart, a real `/usage` or ChatGPT answer. If the poll is slow (polls go one account after another, a Claude `/usage` may take a minute), the chain starts a little later; if it fails once, the chain waits until the next poll five minutes later. **Workaround:** none needed, the chain goes on by itself; two failed polls in a row let it go on without limits.

## Limits kept across a restart (BTN-631)

The newest limits a poll or an SDK session told are kept in the state database and are the starting value after a restart; younger than one poll interval they also lift the wait above, older ones are shown muted as outdated with their age until a poll answers. Covered by tests and the fake page (`?account=outdated`); an end-to-end scenario restarts the service on the same state database (`src/e2e/limits.test.ts`, BTN-663): a snapshot seconds old lets the next task start before the first poll answers, one ten minutes old is shown outdated on the account's card without the window that reset meanwhile, and the mark goes once the poll answers. Seen after a real restart (2026-11-02, [verified.md](../verified/claude-limits.md)): every account's figures, three minutes old, were in the state from its first second and gave way to each account's first poll 5–40 s later. Seen after a restart with the service down 5.5 min (2026-11-07, [verified.md](../verified/claude-limits.md)): the cards showed the 10-minute-old figures muted with "not checked since the restart", and the mark went once each poll answered. Not seen: a task started on a kept snapshot before the first poll. If it is wrong, the page shows an old statusline figure again, or the outdated mark stays after the poll has answered, or a task starts on a saved figure that a window's reset has made stale. **Workaround:** wait for the next poll (five minutes); the page and the chain follow it.

## Plans without a five-hour or an all-models window (BTN-729)

A verified Claude Ultra has no five-hour window and a Team or Enterprise seat may have no all-models week; `/usage` then leaves that line out, and the account is read from the line it has, without a meter for the missing window (`docs/sessions/limits.md`). Covered by tests on answers built from what users reported of "Settings → Usage" and `/api/oauth/usage` (`src/limits/usage.test.ts`); no such account was at hand, and the exact `/usage` text of either was never seen. If the line is worded otherwise than "Current week (all models)" or "Current session", or a missing window comes as `0% used` instead, the account stays unread with the `/usage` warning, or shows an empty five-hour meter. **Check:** on a verified Ultra and on a Team seat, the account's card shows only the windows the plan has, with no `/usage` warning, and the log has no `usage` error. **Workaround:** none; a limit is still caught by the session's refusal.

## A model's own week from the `/usage` stream (BTN-805)

The `/usage` poll asks for `--output-format stream-json --verbose` instead of `json` and reads the model weeks (Fable) from the `usage_report` of the `/usage` message (`docs/sessions/limits.md`). The stream itself is read live on Pro accounts: their limits come in with an empty list of model weeks and no `usage` warning ([verified](../verified/claude-limits.md)). Not seen: a plan that has a model week. The rows are tested on answers users posted of Max, Ultra and Team (`test/fixtures/limits/`); if their message carries no `usage_report`, no model week is shown, and the plan's windows are read as before. **Check:** on a Max, Ultra or Team account, the row shows "Fable week". **Workaround:** none needed for the plan's windows; a spent model week is caught by the session's refusal.

## A spent model week holds the chain (BTN-816)

A model's own week (Fable on Max, Ultra and Team) used up holds the next task whose model is that one: the task waits until the week opens, while a task on another model of the same plan goes on (`docs/sessions/limits.md`, "A model's own week"). The server names the week by its `display_name` ("Fable") and the task's model is what the CLI takes ("fable", "claude-fable-5"), so the two are matched by the family they name. Covered by tests and by an end-to-end scenario with the scripted agent's `/usage` (`src/e2e/limits.test.ts`): a week at 100 % holds the task, and the task starts once a poll shows the week open. Not seen: a plan with a model week at all — no Max, Ultra or Team account was at hand. If the server names a week by a word the model does not carry (a rename, a version in the name), the week holds nothing and the task starts into the refusal as before; if the name carries a word of another model, that task would wait for a week that is not its own. A week the server gave no reset for has no known end: the task waits and only a poll can tell that the week opened. **Check:** on a Max, Ultra or Team account, a task on Fable waits while the Fable week is at 100 %, and a task on another model of the plan goes on. **Workaround:** switch the account, or take the model off the task's label.

## Fast mode in statistics

Responses in fast mode are recorded (`speed = fast`), but none have appeared in the data, so `/stats` prices them as normal responses and their cost is understated (`docs/tally.md`).
